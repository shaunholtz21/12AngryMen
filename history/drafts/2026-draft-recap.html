📝 2026 Draft Recap

Season Kickoff • Draft Room Chaos

Twelve managers entered the draft room. Twelve managers left convinced they'd found a sleeper or two. Here's how the 2026 squads shaped up.

🎯 The Inevitable Champions – Stars and Scrubs, Refined

Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Brock Bowers in the first two rounds is about as clean a 1-2 punch as it gets — elite WR1, elite TE1, no apologies. After that it's less "superstar assembly" and more "collect useful humans": Pickens gives them a real WR2, Etienne and Swift form a low-ceiling-but-reliable RB corps, and Dobbins/Harvey/Rodriguez round out the room with three different flavors of "if the starter gets hurt." Dak and Caleb is a fine QB room without being flashy, and Ferguson gives Bowers actual insurance at TE. Nothing here screams boom, but nothing screams bust either.

Projection: Floor over ceiling. This team probably doesn't blow anyone out, but it also probably doesn't get blown out — a name that might end up ironic if the depth pieces don't hit, or extremely on-brand if they do.

❓ Shoulda Played The Other Guy – Running It Back

Jonathan Taylor and Derrick Henry to open the draft is about as "we are drafting a ground-and-pound offense" as it gets — RB1 and RB1B locked in before anyone else picked a receiver. Smith and Egbuka give them a legit WR1/WR2, and LaPorta is a quiet steal at TE in round 5. The RB room stays deep with Warren, Gainwell, and Mason mixed in, but the QB situation is Bo Nix and Sam Darnold, which is a "please don't get hurt" plan more than a plan. WR depth thins out fast after round 4 into names you stream more than start.

Projection: Two workhorse backs can carry a fantasy team a long way — if Taylor and Henry both stay upright, the name might finally stop being ironic. If either goes down, the receiver corps isn't built to pick up the slack.

🧨 HuPhers – The WR Avalanche Strikes Again

Once again, wide receivers everywhere. Flowers, McConkey, Odunze, Harrison Jr., and Reed is five legitimate pass-catchers before round 9, which is either a championship-caliber receiving corps or a fantasy points traffic jam depending on the week. Chase Brown and Hampton give them real RB1/RB2 juice early, which is new — last year's version of this team was praying the RBs showed up at all. Mahomes at pick 110 is a genuine steal, and Kraft gives them a sneaky-good TE. The QB room gets weird late with Daniel Jones as the backup plan, but with Mahomes throwing to this many weapons, that shouldn't matter much.

Projection: Still boom-or-bust by identity, but with better bones this year. If Mahomes stays healthy and even three of these five receivers pop, this team could actually finish top of the league in points instead of just top of the league in chaos.

💥 Hooligans – The Complete Package

McCaffrey at pick 8 is the kind of "if healthy" superstar who can single-handedly win weeks, and London gives them a true WR1 right behind him. What stands out this year is the balance — Adams, Metcalf, Meyers, and Shakir is a genuinely deep and talented receiver room, not the patchwork unit this team's had in years past. Pollard, Jones Sr., and Spears back up McCaffrey with real handcuff value, and Daniels/Stroud at QB is a two-QB room most teams would be jealous of. Warren gives them a rookie TE with upside, and Johnson's there for depth.

Projection: This is the most complete roster this team has fielded in a while — legit talent at every position with no obvious hole to exploit. The only real risk is health at the top: if McCaffrey misses time, this team goes from "contender" to "solid but unspectacular" fast.

🍻 Dirty Beers – The Rookie Ranch, Reloaded

Lamb and Brown at the top is as good a start as any team in this draft, but after that it's rookie roulette again — Price, McMillan, Coleman, Tate, and Stribling is five first/second-year names drafted on pure hope. The TE room is bizarrely stacked with Goedert, Andrews, and Henry, like they couldn't stop themselves once they started, and then they drafted two kickers, which nobody asked for. Meanwhile the RB room is Price, Coleman, and Pacheco at pick 186 — thin doesn't begin to cover it.

Projection: Lamb and Brown alone can keep this team afloat some weeks, but with almost no proven running back and a bench full of unproven rookies, this is a classic feast-or-famine build. If two or three of the young names break out, watch out. If not, it's a long year propped up by two elite WRs and a surplus of tight ends nobody needs.

🔢 Hankson – The Balanced Risk-Taker, Still Balanced

Chase at pick 4 is about as safe a WR1 as exists, and Jeanty gives them a true bell-cow RB1 right after — a strong, low-drama start. From there it's the same identity as always: a little bit of everything. Olave and McLaurin round out a solid WR corps, Lamar gives them rushing upside at QB most teams don't get, and Stevenson/Hubbard/Monangai keep the RB room deep if unspectacular. Kincaid is a nice value at TE, and Deebo adds a wildcard who can line up anywhere.

Projection: No glaring holes, no huge reaches — the same steady, well-rounded build that's made this team a perennial playoff threat. Lamar's rushing floor and Chase's target share give this roster a higher ceiling than "balanced" usually implies.

🧱 The Max – Blueprint Perfectionist, Same Energy

Nacua and Josh Allen through rounds 1-3 is about as clean a foundation as this draft has — true WR1 talent plus a QB who can win weeks with his legs alone. From there it's the same spreadsheet-and-vibe-check approach as always: the RB room goes six deep (Love, Skattebo, Irving, Holani, Allgeier, Charbonnet), so bye weeks and injuries barely register. Diggs and Godwin add proven target volume behind Nacua, and Pitts is a classic buy-low TE bet. The one real head-scratcher is Aubrey the kicker at pick 99 — a full round earlier than anyone needed to take a kicker — but that's about the only thing resembling a mistake on this roster.

Projection: Deep at every skill position with a true difference-maker at QB and WR1. If this team loses, it's probably going to be because of that early kicker pick, not the roster construction.

🧊 Baune – From Upside Bet to Stacked

Jefferson and Achane at picks 12 and 13 is about as good a WR1/RB1 combo as this league saw fall to one team, and Nabers plus Judkins in rounds 3-4 keeps the hits coming. This isn't the youth-and-hope gamble from last season — this is a roster with legitimate stars at the top. Burrow anchors the QB room, and the two-TE approach continues with Fannin and Likely, though Fannin looks like the real difference-maker there. Dowdle and Sampson give the RB room depth behind Achane, and yes, two kickers made the roster again, which at this point might just be a team tradition.

Projection: The talent ceiling here is real championship contender — Jefferson and Achane alone can win a week outright. If the depth pieces (Pierce, Lemon, the second TE) contribute even a little, this could be the best version of this team yet.

🔐 The Prison Wallets – Breaking Out of Ground Game Jail

Bijan Robinson at pick 2 is a true RB1 anchor, and Kyren Williams gives them a second workhorse right behind him — the run-heavy identity is still here, but this year it's paired with real receiver talent instead of hoping for the best. Watson, Moore, Sutton, Golden, and Doubs is a surprisingly deep and talented WR room, a big departure from a roster that used to be RBs-or-bust. Hurts adds rushing upside at QB, and Kelce, even at this stage of his career, is still a trustworthy TE1. Jacobs and Marks add RB depth, and Freiermuth is a solid TE2 if Kelce needs a breather.

Projection: This team finally has the receiver help it always lacked — pairing that with a strong two-back rotation and a rushing QB gives it one of the more well-rounded builds in the league this year. The "trade RBs like NFTs" era might officially be over.

🧠 Corbett – Air Raid Architect, Now With a Real RB1

St. Brown, Rice, Higgins, Addison, Worthy, Jeudy, and Dell — this is still the deepest, most stacked wide receiver room in the league by a wide margin. But the real story is Saquon Barkley falling to pick 19, giving this team something last year's version never had: a legitimate feature back instead of just "whoever's left in the flex." Herbert and Goff give them two capable arms to spread it around, and Kittle plus Hockenson makes the TE room genuinely dangerous too. Tuten and White fill out the RB room behind Barkley.

Projection: If last year's Corbett team needed the RBs to "stay functional," this year they might not need to — Barkley alone changes the equation. Combined with a receiver corps this deep, this looks like the team to beat if the health gods cooperate.

⚡ Team Spreitzer – The RB Hoarder

Cook III at pick 10 isn't a marquee name, but this team backed it up with volume — Montgomery, Henderson, Croskey-Merritt, Kamara, and Washington Jr. round out a six-deep running back room that could survive a bye-week apocalypse on its own. McBride in round 2 is a legitimate TE1 steal, and Wilson/Waddle give them real receiver juice up top, with Hunter and Allen adding depth. The QB room is Stafford and Purdy — both capable game managers rather than difference-makers, which about sums up this whole draft: no big swings, just quietly collecting useful players at every spot.

Projection: This roster doesn't have a signature superstar carrying it, but the sheer running back depth means matchup problems and injuries barely register. A quiet, sturdy build that could sneak into the playoffs on volume alone.

🌿 Team Greenwell – High Variance, Now With a No. 1 Pick

Gibbs first overall is about as safe a top pick as this format allows, and Walker III right after gives them a genuinely strong RB1/RB2 combo. Collins, Burden, Evans, Thomas Jr., and Johnston stack up a deep and talented WR room, while Loveland gives them rookie TE upside. Hall and Brooks add RB depth in case of injury, and the QB room (Lawrence, Love) is fine without being a strength. This still has some of that classic Greenwell boom-or-bust DNA — a lot of unproven or streaky names mixed in with the stars — but the floor is much higher than past years thanks to that first pick.

Projection: Still capable of running away with the league or stumbling into a .500 season depending on which sleepers hit, but starting from Gibbs at 1.01 gives this team a much sturdier foundation than "wildcard" usually implies.
