📝 2022 Draft Recap

Season Kickoff • Draft Room Chaos

Twelve managers entered the draft room in the middle of the wildest offseason of trades in recent memory. Here's how the 2022 squads shaped up.

🔥 Dumpster Fire – The Post-Adams Rodgers Bet

Dalvin Cook at pick 5 carries the injury-prone label he's never quite shaken, and CeeDee Lamb steps into a bigger role after Dallas traded away Amari Cooper. The headline pick is Aaron Rodgers in round 7 — the back-to-back reigning MVP, but now without Davante Adams for the first time in years, leaning on a much younger receiving corps. Nick Chubb gives them a stable floor at running back.

Projection: Chubb and Lamb are the safe floor. Rodgers without Adams is the single biggest question mark on this roster.

🧱 The Max – The Safest Pick in the Draft

Jonathan Taylor at pick 2 is going #1 overall in a lot of rankings this summer after an MVP-caliber, league-leading rushing season in 2021 — about as close to a sure thing as fantasy football offers. Mark Andrews and Patrick Mahomes round out a strong top three. The receiver room outside of Diontae Johnson and Marquise Brown is thin, and there's real workload risk for Taylor given how many touches he saw last year.

Projection: If Taylor repeats anywhere close to last year, this roster is a contender out of the gate.

🧊 Team Baune – The Triple Crown Pick

Cooper Kupp at pick 3 comes off winning the receiving Triple Crown in 2021 — leading the NFL in catches, yards, and touchdowns — and is a consensus top-five pick this summer. Aaron Jones gives them a strong RB1, and Josh Jacobs adds a second capable back. Matthew Stafford, fresh off a Super Bowl, anchors the QB room. Depth thins fast outside the top four.

Projection: Elite at the top with Kupp and Jones. If Kupp can repeat anywhere near his 2021 numbers, this team is a real threat.

🐕 The Dog House – Cashing In on the Tyreek Trade

Austin Ekeler at pick 4 is a proven three-down back, and Tyreek Hill at pick 21 is the single biggest storyline of the offseason — traded from Kansas City to Miami, now pairing with Jaylen Waddle in an offense many expect to fly. Terry McLaurin and DK Metcalf add real receiver depth. Dak Prescott, coming back from a broken ankle, is a bit of a wild card at QB.

Projection: If Hill's move to Miami pays off the way the hype suggests, this team has one of the highest ceilings in the league.

⚓ Navy Bombers – Betting the Drama Resolves Well

Joe Mixon anchors the backfield, and Deebo Samuel — who requested a trade this offseason before ultimately re-signing with San Francisco on a three-year extension — arrives with some off-field noise but all his 2021 All-Pro talent intact. Tee Higgins and DeVonta Smith add real receiver depth, and Kyler Murray anchors the QB room. Tony Pollard is a nice value flier in the Dallas backfield.

Projection: If the Samuel situation is truly settled, this team has one of the more talented receiver rooms in the league.

🌿 Team Greenwell – The Injury-History Special

Christian McCaffrey at pick 1 is the single riskiest #1 overall pick in years — he's played just 10 games combined over the past two seasons, making this a true buy-low bet on a generational talent staying healthy. Saquon Barkley, coming off his own injury-plagued stretch, doubles down on that risk at pick 24. Amon-Ra St. Brown and Lamar Jackson add real weekly upside if the injury luck turns around.

Projection: The single highest-variance roster in the league. If McCaffrey and Barkley both stay on the field, nobody beats this team. If not, it's a long year.

🧠 1112 Aaww Zek Zek – The Brown Trade Bet

Ja'Marr Chase at pick 7 comes off a sensational rookie season reunited with Joe Burrow, and expectations are sky-high for year two. A.J. Brown, traded from Tennessee to Philadelphia on draft night in one of the offseason's biggest moves, gives them a second true WR1 talent now catching passes from Jalen Hurts. D'Andre Swift and George Kittle round out a strong core. Tom Brady in round 7 is a proven, if aging, floor at QB.

Projection: Two legitimate WR1s is a rare luxury. If either rookie situation (Brown in Philly) clicks fast, this team is dangerous.

🍻 Dirty Beers – The Consensus QB1

Derrick Henry at pick 8 gives them a true workhorse, and Josh Allen at pick 17 is the unanimous top quarterback in nearly every ranking this summer — no other passer is even in his tier. Chris Olave and Garrett Wilson add rookie WR upside from a loaded 2022 class, and Breece Hall gives them a second exciting rookie back. Trevor Lawrence, entering year two, is a nice value QB2.

Projection: Henry and Allen form an elite floor. If either rookie receiver hits early, this team has real teeth.

🌴 The Salamanders – Banking on a Bell Cow

Najee Harris at pick 9 comes off a workhorse rookie season with a massive workload, and Stefon Diggs gives them a locked-in WR1. Joe Burrow, fresh off a Super Bowl run, anchors the QB room at a nice discount in round 7. The receiver depth thins fast past Diggs and Godwin, and this roster leans on a lot of name-recognition RBs (Elliott, Dobbins, Mostert) coming off down or injury-shortened years.

Projection: Harris and Diggs give this team a strong floor. The bounce-back bets at running back are the swing factor.

⚙️ The Jerminator – The Kamara Gamble

Alvin Kamara at pick 10 is a proven RB1, but he's carrying real risk into the season with a felony battery case still working through the courts — a potential suspension looms, even if it may not land until 2023. Javonte Williams and Kyle Pitts add real upside, and Jalen Hurts gives them rushing floor at QB. Russell Wilson as a round-8 flier, fresh off his blockbuster trade to Denver, adds late-round intrigue.

Projection: High upside if Kamara avoids off-field trouble. The legal situation is the one thing this team can't control.

🔢 Team Hankson – The Adams Reunion

Davante Adams at pick 12 is the headline: traded from Green Bay to Las Vegas this offseason to reunite with his college quarterback Derek Carr, one of the most talked-about moves of the spring. Travis Kelce locks down tight end, and Justin Herbert anchors a strong QB room. The running back room (Montgomery, Singletary, Gibson) is committee-heavy without a clear standout.

Projection: If the Adams-Carr chemistry translates immediately, this team has a true WR1 anchor. The backfield needs someone to separate from the pack.

💥 The Hooligans – Building Around a Rising Star

Justin Jefferson at pick 6 is coming off back-to-back 1,400-yard seasons to open his career and is squarely in the WR1 conversation. Mike Evans and Mike Williams add proven volume, but the running back room (Conner, Edwards-Helaire, Hunt) is full of committee question marks, and Kirk Cousins/Trey Lance is a shaky QB room to build a contender around.

Projection: Jefferson alone can carry weeks. The RB room needs a clear lead back to emerge for this team to really compete.
