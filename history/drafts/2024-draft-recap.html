📝 2024 Draft Recap

Season Kickoff • Draft Room Chaos

Twelve managers entered the draft room. Twelve managers left convinced they'd outsmarted everyone else at the table. Here's how the 2024 squads shaped up.

🔥 Dumpster Fire – Ironically Well-Built

Tyreek Hill at pick 1 is the single best asset in this draft, and Deebo Samuel plus Josh Jacobs give them real juice at WR2 and RB1. Malik Nabers is a bold rookie swing at pick 48 with real upside, and Joe Mixon adds a second capable back. The name says disaster, but the roster says otherwise — this is a sneaky-good build.

Projection: The name might age poorly. Hill alone can win weeks, and there's enough talent around him to make noise all season.

🧱 The Max – The Safe Bet

Christian McCaffrey at pick 2 is as close to a sure thing as fantasy football offers, and Travis Kelce in round 3 gives them a second locked-in stud. Chris Olave and Stefon Diggs add real receiver volume, and the RB room stays deep with Aaron Jones and Zamir White behind McCaffrey. A kicker at pick 95 is a full round early, but that's the only real nitpick on an otherwise clean build.

Projection: Top-heavy stars plus solid depth. If McCaffrey stays on the field, this is a contender start to finish.

⚓ Navy Bombers – Ground and Pound

Bijan Robinson at pick 3 is a true bell-cow anchor, and Sam LaPorta gives them a locked-in TE1 by round 3. Joe Burrow at pick 51 is a strong value at QB, and Rachaad White adds a second capable back behind Robinson. The receiver room past Marvin Harrison Jr. gets thin fast — Mike Williams, Tyler Lockett, and Darnell Mooney are all more WR3 than difference-maker at this stage.

Projection: Strong at the positions that matter most. If one more receiver steps up, this team has real playoff teeth.

⚙️ The Jerminator – The Efficient Machine

Lamb and Adams at picks 4 and 21 is about as good a 1-2 WR punch as this draft offered, and Kamara plus Kenneth Walker III gives them real juice at running back too. Waddle and Pitts round out a genuinely balanced skill-position core, and grabbing Jayden Daniels as a QB2 flier in round 9 is exactly the kind of low-cost rookie bet a deep roster can afford.

Projection: No obvious hole in this build. If the rookie QB pick turns into anything at all, it's just gravy on an already-solid roster.

🧊 Team Baune – The Rushing QB Bet

Amon-Ra St. Brown at pick 5 is a clean WR1, and Lamar Jackson right after gives them the rushing floor most QBs can't offer. Etienne Jr. and DJ Moore round out a solid top four, and Jake Ferguson is a nice value at tight end. The bench gets speculative fast — Jameson Williams and Bucky Irving are pure lottery tickets — but the starting core is genuinely strong.

Projection: Lamar's rushing upside gives this team a higher weekly floor than most QB-led rosters. Depth is the concern if the top four hit any bumps.

💥 Hooligans – Stacked and Loaded

Ja'Marr Chase at pick 6 is a true WR1 anchor, and Kyren Williams gives them a legitimate RB1 right behind him. Mike Evans, DK Metcalf, and Chris Godwin in rounds 3-5 is an absurd amount of proven receiver talent to stack on top of that, and Brock Bowers adds rookie TE upside. This is about as complete a roster as anyone built this draft.

Projection: Championship-caliber on paper. The only question is whether there's enough room in the lineup to use all these receivers at once.

🧠 Team Corbett – The Air Raid Returns

Justin Jefferson at pick 7 is a top-tier anchor, and Michael Pittman Jr. plus Tee Higgins give this team serious receiver depth. James Cook adds a real RB1, and George Kittle is a proven difference-maker at tight end. Aaron Rodgers at pick 79 is a low-cost, high-name-value flier at QB2 — either a steal or a non-factor, with little in between.

Projection: Deep and talented at the skill positions. If Rodgers has anything left, this roster has no real weaknesses.

🔢 Team Hankson – The Boom-or-Bust Gambit

Breece Hall and Puka Nacua to open is a strong, if slightly injury-prone, foundation. Derrick Henry adds a bruising RB2, and Cooper Kupp gives them a proven second receiver — assuming his body cooperates. Anthony Richardson at pick 41 is the wildest swing of the draft: electric upside, zero track record of consistency. This roster lives and dies on health and Richardson figuring it out.

Projection: Highest ceiling in the league if everything breaks right. Also one of the most fragile rosters if it doesn't.

🍻 Dirty Beers – The QB Hoarders

Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes in the first two rounds, then Dak Prescott in round 3 — three legitimate starting QBs before anyone else at the table finished their first cup of coffee. That leaves the skill positions thin: Calvin Ridley and Jaxon Smith-Njigba are fine, but the running back room (Harris, Spears, Chubb, Charbonnet) is speculative at best, and two kickers made the roster, which nobody needed.

Projection: Elite at quarterback, shaky everywhere else. This team wins shootouts and loses grind-it-out weeks.

🌴 The Salamanders – The Eagles Special

A.J. Brown and Jalen Hurts back-to-back is a full-on Philly investment, and stacking Mark Andrews and Trey McBride at tight end in rounds 4-5 is either genius or a hoarding problem. C.J. Stroud gives them a second capable arm, but the running back room (Montgomery, Harris, Moss, Allgeier, Chandler) is committee city — nobody here looks like a clear bell cow.

Projection: If Brown stays the focal point of that Eagles offense, this team has a real floor. The RB-by-committee approach means they'll be streaming matchups all year.

🧨 HuPhers – Running Backs First, Ask Questions Later

Jonathan Taylor and Jahmyr Gibbs in the first two rounds is a statement: this team is winning or losing on the ground. Aiyuk and DeVonta Smith give them solid, if unspectacular, receiver depth, and Brock Purdy at pick 83 is a nice value play at QB. The back half of the roster is thin at impact talent, but the top of this roster hits as hard as anyone's.

Projection: Taylor and Gibbs alone could carry this team to the playoffs if both stay healthy. If either goes down, there's not much behind them to pick up the slack.

🌿 Team Greenwell – Betting on the Come-Up

Garrett Wilson and Saquon Barkley at picks 12 and 13 is a legitimate WR1/RB1 combo to build around, and De'Von Achane plus Nico Collins add serious upside in rounds 3-4. Caleb Williams as the QB1 is a rookie-year unknown, and the roster leans heavily on unproven or first-year names (Rice, Njoku, Brooks) to round things out. High talent, high uncertainty.

Projection: The pieces are undeniably talented. Whether this team contends or scuffles probably comes down to how fast the rookie quarterback grows up.
